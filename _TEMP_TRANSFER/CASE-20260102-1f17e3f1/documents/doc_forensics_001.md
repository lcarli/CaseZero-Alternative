# Evidence Examination Report: Deposit Bag Seal Material and Safe-Area Condition Documentation

## Laboratory and Examiner Information

**Laboratory:** Forensic Services Unit (FSU), Evidence Processing Section  
**Examiner:** Forensic Technician (FSU)  
**Report Date/Time:** 2026-01-02T11:00:00-05:00  
**Case References:** Timeline items E003, E004, E009; associated report docId doc_forensics_001.


## Case Summary and Request

FSU received a request to document and examine (1) the deposit bag seal material and any visible seal condition issues, and (2) the condition of a designated safe-area surface/fixtures as recorded in submitted materials. The requested scope was limited to basic physical examination, photography, and non-destructive comparisons among submitted items.

Items submitted for this report include EV005, EV006, and EV011. The purpose of the examination was to record observable features (e.g., seal condition, signs of handling, surface disturbance) and to preserve those observations in a consistent format for later investigative and courtroom use. This report does not make conclusions about who handled any item.


## Evidence Description

- **EV005:** Deposit bag/seal material submitted for examination. Packaging observed to be sealed upon receipt.  
- **EV006:** Additional deposit bag/seal-related material or associated packaging submitted for comparison/documentation. Packaging observed to be sealed upon receipt.  
- **EV011:** Condition documentation item(s) associated with the safe-area (e.g., images or recorded condition notes) submitted for review. Packaging observed to be sealed upon receipt.

No destructive testing was requested. Any markings, labels, or identifiers present on the submitted items were treated as part of the evidence record and photographed as received.


## Methodology

All work was completed under standard FSU evidence handling practices.

1. **Intake and verification:** Verified evidence identifiers (EV005, EV006, EV011) against the submission paperwork and referenced timeline points (E003, E004, E009) for general sequencing.  
2. **Photography:** Conducted overall and close-range photographs of each item as received, including seal edges, adhesive areas, and any visible surface disturbance.  
3. **Visual examination:** Performed a systematic visual scan under white light to note:  
   - Seal continuity and alignment  
   - Presence of folds, creases, or stretch marks  
   - Adhesive edge condition (lifting, separation, uneven bonding)  
   - Surface debris and general cleanliness (non-collection, observation only)  
4. **Low-magnification review:** Used low magnification to document micro-features such as minor abrasions, edge fray, and localized adhesive texture differences.  
5. **Non-destructive comparison:** Compared EV005 and EV006 for general material class similarities (appearance, thickness impression, adhesive edge look) without altering or sampling any material.  
6. **Safe-area condition review:** Reviewed EV011 for observable condition indicators (e.g., presence/absence of visible disturbance) and documented notable features as descriptive notes.


## Results

- **EV005:** The seal material and associated edges were photographed and documented. Visual examination noted normal handling-type features (minor creasing and edge contact marks) consistent with routine movement and storage. No cutting, tearing, or gross material loss was observed during this examination.  
- **EV006:** Photographs and notes were completed. The material appeared generally consistent with EV005 in overall type/appearance at the non-destructive level. Minor surface scuffs and localized pressure impressions were observed. No obvious perforations or separated layers were observed.  
- **EV011:** The submitted safe-area condition documentation was reviewed as provided. The images/recorded condition information showed clear views of the relevant surfaces sufficient for basic documentation. No additional measurements could be taken beyond what is visible in EV011.

All observations above are limited to what was visible and documentable without altering evidence.


## Interpretation

The documented features on EV005 and EV006 are consistent with items that have been handled and stored. The examination did not include chemical testing of adhesives, latent print development, or trace collection; therefore, the results only support a basic description of condition.

The review of EV011 provides a record of the safe-area condition as captured at the time of documentation. Those materials are suitable for supporting a timeline narrative (see E003, E004, E009) in terms of “what was visible,” but they do not, by themselves, identify a handler or establish intent. No conclusion is offered regarding how, when, or by whom any condition changes occurred.
