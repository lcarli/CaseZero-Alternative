## Case Summary and Request

Per case timeline entries **E007**, **E008**, and **E009**, investigators requested a basic assessment for potential friction ridge detail (latent fingerprints) and observable handling indicators on supply-area-related items.

Requested actions:
1. Screen selected items for visible residue, smears, or handling artifacts.
2. Conduct a basic latent print development sequence suitable for mixed surfaces.
3. Document any developed ridge detail and note suitability for comparison.

This report addresses only the evidence received under **EV010**, **EV011**, and **EV009**. No conclusions are offered regarding any person’s involvement.

## Evidence Description

The following items were examined as received:

- **EV010:** Submitted touchpoint item(s) associated with the supply-area (packaged). Outer packaging intact on receipt.
- **EV011:** Submitted materials associated with supply-area handling/transport (packaged). Outer packaging intact on receipt.
- **EV009:** Submitted supply-area item(s) identified by investigators for possible handling contact (packaged). Outer packaging intact on receipt.

Condition notes at receipt: Packaging showed no obvious tears or compromised seals. Items were not cleaned prior to examination.

## Methodology

Procedures were performed using standard laboratory latent print practices appropriate for a basic screen:

1. **Intake and documentation:** Items **EV010**, **EV011**, and **EV009** were photographed in packaging, then after removal, with attention to likely contact surfaces (edges, grips, smooth panels).
2. **Visual assessment:** White light and oblique lighting were used to locate visible smears, residue, or ridge detail.
3. **Powder processing (non-porous areas):** Black latent powder applied with a fiberglass brush where suitable; lifts made with transparent lifting tape and secured on backing cards.
4. **Cyanoacrylate (CA) fuming screen (non-porous areas):** A controlled CA fuming cycle was used where powder results were limited, followed by re-examination under alternate lighting.
5. **Documentation:** Any developed impressions were photographed with scale and labeled to the source item.

No chemical processing intended for porous substrates (e.g., amino acid reagents) was conducted in this basic assessment.

## Results

- **EV010:** One area of apparent handling smear was noted. Powder processing produced partial ridge flow in one localized region; ridge detail was **limited** and did not show sufficient minutiae for a reliable comparison. No additional suitable impressions observed after CA fuming screen.

- **EV011:** Multiple contact areas were examined. Several powder-developed marks were consistent with **handling smudges**; one partial impression displayed ridge characteristics but remained **fragmentary**. CA fuming did not enhance ridge clarity to comparison quality.

- **EV009:** Visual/oblique exam identified general handling residue on contact surfaces. Powder processing yielded no liftable ridge detail. CA fuming produced faint development consistent with surface contamination; no distinct ridge detail suitable for comparison was observed.

All lifts and photographs were retained with the case file under their parent evidence items (**EV010**, **EV011**, **EV009**).

## Interpretation

The examinations of **EV010**, **EV011**, and **EV009** identified indications of handling (smears/residue) but did **not** produce fingerprint impressions of sufficient quality for comparison.

Possible reasons include:
- Surface conditions (texture, contamination, or prior contact) reducing ridge clarity.
- Pressure/drag during handling creating smudging rather than discrete ridge detail.
- Time and environmental exposure consistent with the sequence described in **E007–E009**.

These results do not indicate who handled the items and should not be interpreted as excluding handling by any individual.

## Limitations

- This was a **basic** latent print assessment and did not include advanced or item-specific chemical sequences for porous substrates.
- Negative or limited findings do not prove an item was not handled; they indicate only that suitable ridge detail was not developed under the methods used.
- Some surfaces may be unsuitable for latent print recovery due to material properties, contamination, or prior wiping.
- No fingerprint comparisons or identifications were performed as part of this report.
